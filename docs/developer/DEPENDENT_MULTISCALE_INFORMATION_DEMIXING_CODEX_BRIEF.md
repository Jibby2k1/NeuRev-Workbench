## 0. Executive directive

Implement a new, reversible, label-free NeuRev experiment that decomposes the Spon Ca Burst movie using **dependent multiscale subspaces** rather than independent scalar components.

The first implementation must test the hypothesis that:

1. compact spatial supports near **5 and 7 pixels** share neural evidence;
2. a broader support near **15 pixels** supplies contextual, background, crowding, or artifact evidence;
3. scale responses generated by the same physical source should remain statistically dependent;
4. dependence should be reduced only between inappropriate semantic groups, such as neural signal and nuisance background after measured nuisance variables are accounted for;
5. the final joint residual across scales should match the joint quiet-acquisition distribution before it is called measurement noise.

The v1 scientific object is one original-space decomposition:

\[
X = \widehat B + \widehat S + \widehat A + \widehat N
    + E_{\mathrm{closure}},
\]

where:

- `background` \(\widehat B\) is persistent, slowly varying, or spatially broad structure;
- `structured_signal` \(\widehat S\) is accepted neural fluorescence;
- `structured_artifact` \(\widehat A\) contains coherent nuisance structure such as motion edges or acquisition artifacts;
- `noise_candidate` \(\widehat N\) is unexplained residual structure and must not be renamed measurement noise until it passes quiet-noise qualification;
- `closure_residual` \(E_{\mathrm{closure}}\) is numerical reconstruction error.

The multiple scales are observations of these shared physical channels. They are **not** separate physical outputs. Do not write independent “5-pixel signal,” “7-pixel signal,” and “15-pixel signal” movies and treat them as different biological sources.

The first implementation must be:

- linear;
- patchwise and overlap-add;
- deterministic under declared seeds;
- reversible in original observation coordinates;
- initialized by local PCA or imported local-PCA factors;
- compatible with a PMD baseline;
- fit without neuron labels;
- evaluated against synthetic, semi-synthetic, sparse-positive real labels, and an exhaustively reviewed bounded field when available.

Do **not** begin with nonlinear encoders, per-neuron ICA, a wide hyperparameter sweep, recursive learned background feedback, or replacement of the accepted scientific carrier.

## 2. Scientific anchors from the existing repository

Every new report must state the following before presenting new results.

### 2.1 Pairwise ICA is not the target

Adjacent-frame InfoMax and CS-Parzen fits approximately rediscovered the temporal difference direction. Pairwise separation and derivative fusion did not replace Raw Direct. Therefore, the proposed method must not reduce the movie to independent adjacent-frame changes.

### 2.2 Amplitude-preserving low-rank structure is useful but not yet decisive

The completed representation benchmark found a small provisional gain for amplitude PCA rank 8. Low-rank spatial ICA was stable at rank 16 but did not win at the fixed candidate budget; high-rank ICA became unstable. Local low-rank representation is therefore a reasonable initialization, not evidence that PCA components are physical sources.

### 2.3 Quiet-relative local information is the strongest direct ITL evidence

The completed patch-information study found that same-location quiet-relative Cauchy--Schwarz divergence at a compact support was more useful than local information potential or positive center-to-neighborhood correntropy as standalone evidence. The 7-pixel CS family was selected in all four outer folds.

### 2.4 Broad scales are context, not standalone signal

The multiscale study found:

- compact 5--7 pixel supports were strongest as standalone evidence;
- standalone performance declined substantially for 11--15 pixel supports;
- 5/7 agreement and 7-minus-15 contrast were the most useful multiscale families;
- broad support helped qualify or suppress compact evidence rather than acting as the principal detector.

### 2.5 Constant global mixtures are saturated

The innovation-denoising program found that local PSD-Wiener, cross-scale consensus, and asymmetric dynamics occupied different useful parts of the frontier, but constant mixtures did not combine those strengths. The next justified architecture is conditional authority, not another broad grid of constant weights.

### 2.6 The current hierarchical Stage-1 learned separation gate failed

The guarded hierarchical Parzen Stage-1 matrix passed numerical stability but failed scientific validity. Adaptive gain remains the strongest general reference; learned gains were case-specific, unresolved classification failed, and the full patchwise Stage 2 remains incomplete. The new method must not depend on the failed learned recurrent background lane as its sole input.

### 2.7 The scientific carrier and ranking evidence remain separate

Denoising, source-separation, and ITL maps may improve proposal generation or ranking while attenuating amplitude or timing. Until preservation gates pass, retain the accepted carrier as the scientific trace and treat the new decomposition as a candidate reconstruction plus auxiliary evidence.

## 3. Problem statement

Current NeuRev multiscale processing uses hand-designed maps and fusion rules:

\[
F_{\max}(p)=\max_s F_s(p),
\]

\[
F_{\mathrm{agree}}(p)=\sqrt{F_5(p)F_7(p)},
\]

\[
F_{\mathrm{contrast}}(p)=
\max\{F_7(p)-\lambda F_{15}(p),0\}.
\]

These formulas are interpretable and empirically useful, but they do not determine:

- which physical source generated each response;
- how much information is duplicated across scales;
- whether broad evidence is background, a large neural source, crowding, motion, or an acquisition artifact;
- whether the removed information is actually noise;
- whether reconstructed channels close to the original observation;
- whether dependencies between neurons or between scales were incorrectly destroyed.

The new method must convert the existing feature-level insight into a source-attribution model.

The core research question is:

> Can local low-rank multiscale observations be partitioned into dependent neural, background, artifact, and residual subspaces using information-theoretic group constraints, while preserving neural amplitude, timing, morphology, and coordinated activity?

## 4. Terminology and artifact semantics

Use these names consistently in code, configuration, reports, and artifacts.

### 4.1 Observation and carrier

- `observation`: calibrated raw or quiet-normalized movie in original pixel coordinates.
- `scientific_carrier`: the accepted amplitude-preserving activity movie or trace used for scientific timing and amplitude until the new reconstruction passes promotion gates.

### 4.2 Scale view

A `scale_view` is a deterministic transformation of the same observation:

\[
Y^{(s)}=R_sX.
\]

A view is not a physical source.

### 4.3 Source groups

- `neural_group`: multiscale representations assigned to structured neural fluorescence.
- `background_group`: multiscale representations assigned to persistent, broad, or slowly varying structure.
- `artifact_group`: multiscale representations assigned to coherent nuisance structure.
- `private_scale_residual`: structured information available in one scale view that is not explained by the shared groups.

### 4.4 Residual terminology

- `noise_candidate`: unexplained original-space residual before qualification.
- `qualified_measurement_noise`: a residual that passes the declared joint quiet-noise contract.
- `closure_residual`: numerical error after summing all reconstructed channels.

### 4.5 Dependence terminology

- `within_group_dependence`: dependency intentionally retained among elements of one semantic group, including the same source across scales.
- `between_group_dependence`: dependency between semantic groups that may be minimized after measured common causes are included.
- `explained_dependence`: dependence attributed to a common latent or observed variable such as motion, illumination, or population burst drive.
- `dependency_graph`: the explicit contract identifying which dependencies are preserved, explained, or penalized.

### 4.6 Scale signature

For source \(k\), the vector

\[
g_k = [g_k(5),g_k(7),g_k(15)]^\top
\]

is its `scale_signature`. This vector is expected to be dependent across entries because all entries arise from one source.

## 5. Falsifiable hypotheses

The v1 experiment must test these hypotheses explicitly.

### H1 — Compact shared neural evidence

A shared latent subspace across 5- and 7-pixel views preserves more true neural signal than treating those views independently.

### H2 — Broad contextual explanation

A 15-pixel view improves attribution by explaining broad background, crowding, or artifact structure; it should not universally replace compact signal evidence.

### H3 — Groupwise dependence is better than scalar independence

Reducing dependence between semantic groups while preserving dependence within the neural group produces lower signal leakage and better stability than ordinary componentwise ICA.

### H4 — Joint residual matching is necessary

Matching the **joint** multiscale residual distribution to quiet acquisition noise is more valid than independently whitening or Gaussianizing each scale residual.

### H5 — Orthogonality is an incomplete baseline

A JIVE-like shared/private orthogonal decomposition improves over independent PCA, but an information-theoretic group criterion provides additional attribution value when dependencies are nonlinear or non-Gaussian.

### H6 — Conditional dependence control is safer than unconditional independence

Conditioning on measured global intensity, motion, and optional population drive reduces background leakage without suppressing coordinated neural activity.

### H7 — The method improves attribution before it improves detection

The first successful outcome may be lower leakage, more valid residuals, and more stable reconstruction without a large gain in sparse-positive detector recall. Detection improvement is not required to establish the decomposition result, but detector replacement is forbidden without downstream utility and precision evidence.

## 6. Mathematical model

### 6.1 Original-space decomposition

For time \(t\):

\[
X_t = B_t + S_t + A_t + N_t.
\]

A useful structured neural model is:

\[
S_t = F c_t,
\]

where \(F\) contains local spatial footprints and \(c_t\) contains temporal activity.

For calcium data, a bounded first temporal reference may be:

\[
c_t = \Gamma c_{t-1}+u_t,
\qquad u_t\geq 0.
\]

Do not impose this calcium model on voltage data. Voltage extensions must allow signed fast dynamics.

### 6.2 Multiscale observations

For the frozen spatial scales

\[
\mathcal S=\{5,7,15\},
\]

define:

\[
Y_t^{(s)}=R_sX_t.
\]

The first implementation must support deterministic nested support operators. Acceptable v1 operators are:

- local histogram or quiet-relative density support;
- normalized box or Gaussian support;
- local PCA projection;
- imported provider local-PCA projection;
- optional PMD projection as a baseline.

The operator family must be recorded in artifacts and cannot change silently between preflight and run.

### 6.3 Local low-rank initialization

For patch \(p\) and scale \(s\), factorize:

\[
Y_p^{(s)}
\approx
U_p^{(s)}\Sigma_p^{(s)}V_p^{(s)\top}.
\]

Define low-dimensional coordinates:

\[
Z_p^{(s)}=U_p^{(s)}\Sigma_p^{(s)}
\]

or an equivalent explicitly documented orientation.

The rank must be selected without labels using one of:

1. a frozen small rank;
2. a quiet-null or parallel-analysis threshold;
3. a stability threshold across temporal blocks;
4. imported provider rank metadata.

Do not select rank from known neuron recall.

### 6.4 Shared/private semantic factorization

For each patch:

\[
Z_p^{(s)}
=
L_{S,p}^{(s)}H_{S,p}
+
L_{B,p}^{(s)}H_{B,p}
+
L_{A,p}^{(s)}H_{A,p}
+
U_p^{(s)}.
\]

Where:

- \(H_{S,p}\) is the shared neural latent group;
- \(H_{B,p}\) is the shared background latent group;
- \(H_{A,p}\) is the shared artifact latent group;
- \(L_{\cdot,p}^{(s)}\) maps each shared group into scale \(s\);
- \(U_p^{(s)}\) is a private-scale residual.

The first implementation may omit the artifact group only in a declared ablation. The production v1 result must include it or explicitly report `artifact_group_unresolved`.

### 6.5 Optional population hierarchy

Coordinated neurons need not be independent. A later v1 ablation may use:

\[
c_{k,t}=w_k g_t+v_{k,t},
\]

where \(g_t\) is a shared population drive and \(v_{k,t}\) is source-specific activity.

Dependence may be penalized among \(v_k\) conditional on \(g\), but do not force raw neural traces \(c_k\) to be independent.

### 6.6 Dependency graph

The default v1 graph is:

| Relationship | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Same neural source across 5/7/15 views | Preserve and model |
| Different neurons in the same burst | Permit; optionally explain through population drive |
| Background across scales | Preserve and model |
| Artifact across scales | Preserve and model |
| Neural vs background after nuisance conditioning | Penalize |
| Neural vs artifact after nuisance conditioning | Penalize |
| Background vs artifact | Permit or weakly penalize; report ablation |
| Residuals across scales | Match the measured joint quiet distribution |
| Overlapping patch reconstructions | Enforce consistency |

The implementation must serialize this graph. Do not hide it inside hard-coded loss terms.

### 6.7 Nuisance variables

Define optional observed nuisance matrix \(M\) from label-free measurements:

- global frame intensity;
- estimated translational motion;
- estimated rotational motion when available;
- saturation fraction;
- frame index or slow acquisition drift basis;
- optional broad field mean or low-frequency illumination coefficients.

The primary group-separation term should approximate:

\[
I_\alpha(H_S;H_B\mid M)
+
I_\alpha(H_S;H_A\mid M),
\]

not merely unconditional dependence.

If conditional estimation is not implemented in the first code wave, regress or project the declared nuisance variables from the relevant groups before evaluating the dependence term. Record that this is a residualization approximation.

### 6.8 Objective

The reference objective is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J
={}&
\sum_{p,s}
\left\|
Z_p^{(s)}
-
L_{S,p}^{(s)}H_{S,p}
-
L_{B,p}^{(s)}H_{B,p}
-
L_{A,p}^{(s)}H_{A,p}
-
U_p^{(s)}
\right\|_{W_{q,p,s}}^2
\\
&+
\lambda_{SB}\,
\mathcal D_{\alpha}(H_S,H_B\mid M)
+
\lambda_{SA}\,
\mathcal D_{\alpha}(H_S,H_A\mid M)
\\
&+
\lambda_Q\,
D_{\mathrm{CS}}
\left(
P_{U^{(5)},U^{(7)},U^{(15)}},
P_{Q^{(5)},Q^{(7)},Q^{(15)}}
\right)
\\
&+
\lambda_O\mathcal L_{\mathrm{overlap}}
+
\Omega_S+
\Omega_B+
\Omega_A.
\end{aligned}
\]

Where:

- \(W_{q,p,s}\) is quiet-derived weighting;
- \(\mathcal D_\alpha\) is a groupwise matrix-based Rényi dependence measure or a declared proxy;
- \(Q\) is the measured joint quiet residual;
- \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{overlap}}\) enforces neighboring-patch agreement;
- \(\Omega_S\), \(\Omega_B\), and \(\Omega_A\) are explicit structural regularizers.

### 6.9 Structural regularizers

The v1 model should support:

#### Neural group

- compact or annular spatial support;
- bounded total variation;
- nonnegative amplitude for calcium only;
- optional stable temporal dynamics;
- no forced independence among neural traces.

#### Background group

- spatial breadth or low-frequency preference;
- temporal smoothness;
- correlation with global illumination as diagnostic, not a hard requirement;
- no use of event labels.

#### Artifact group

- motion-edge correlation;
- spatial coherence with low temporal persistence or acquisition periodicity;
- explicit `unresolved` behavior when artifact evidence is insufficient.

### 6.10 Information estimators

Implement and compare these in order.

#### Baseline A — independent local PCA

Factor each scale separately and reconstruct without shared groups.

#### Baseline B — orthogonal shared/private decomposition

Use a JIVE-like reconstruction and orthogonality contract. This tests whether shared/private structure alone is useful.

#### Reference ITL — matrix-based Rényi group dependence

For a normalized Gram matrix \(K\):

\[
A=\frac{K}{\operatorname{tr}K},
\qquad
H_\alpha(A)
=
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
\log\operatorname{tr}(A^\alpha).
\]

Use normalized Hadamard products for joint quantities according to the selected matrix-entropy implementation. The default v1 order is \(\alpha=2\), with one nearby sensitivity value only after the reference passes.

The estimator must operate on semantic groups, not every scalar component.

#### Residual ITL — joint Parzen Cauchy--Schwarz divergence

For joint residual distribution \(p\) and quiet reference \(q\):

\[
D_{\mathrm{CS}}(p,q)
=-\log
\frac{V_{pq}^2}{V_{pp}V_{qq}}.
\]

Use bounded kernels or low-dimensional residual coordinates. Do not construct an unbounded quadratic full-pixel Gram matrix.

### 6.11 Original-space reconstruction

Each patch fit must map its semantic groups back through the scale projection and then to original pixel coordinates.

Write patch contributions:

\[
\widehat B_p,
\widehat S_p,
\widehat A_p,
\widehat N_p.
\]

Blend overlapping patches with a declared window and normalize by accumulated weights.

Finally compute:

\[
E_{\mathrm{closure}}
=
X-
\widehat B-
\widehat S-
\widehat A-
\widehat N.
\]

Closure is a numerical integrity test. It is not proof of correct attribution.

## 7. Frozen v1 scope

### 7.1 Spatial scales

Use exactly:

```text
5 px compact
7 px compact
15 px broad context
```

Do not reintroduce 9, 11, or 13 pixels in the first confirmation run. They may be added only after the frozen three-scale model is understood.

### 7.2 Analysis patches

Default reference geometry:

```text
analysis patch: 31 x 31 pixels
stride: 8 pixels
overlap window: separable Hann with positive floor
border mode: reflect
```

These values may be changed during preflight only if the image geometry makes them invalid. Any change must produce a new manifest and output root.

### 7.3 Model class

The v1 model is linear in the local factor coordinates. Nonlinear encoders and decoders are out of scope.

### 7.4 Input lanes

Support:

1. imported provider local-PCA factors;
2. NeuRev-computed local PCA fallback;
3. optional local PMD baseline when the dependency is available and the adapter is bounded;
4. accepted carrier as a parallel scientific trace;
5. raw or calibrated observation for original-space closure.

If the provider supplies only a denoised movie, treat it as an external reconstruction baseline. Do not infer the missing factors.

### 7.5 Fitting supervision

Neuron labels, ROI identities, event coordinates, and event intervals are unavailable to fitting, rank selection, component assignment, and residual qualification.

Labels enter only:

- post-fit evaluation;
- leave-one-burst model selection among a very small preregistered configuration set;
- visualization overlays;
- exhaustive-field precision evaluation.

### 7.6 Execution

- CPU deterministic synthetic and tiny smoke runs are mandatory.
- CUDA may be used for bounded matrix and patch operations only after CPU tests pass.
- A full Spon run requires explicit user selection and a new collision-safe output root.
- No streaming claim is permitted from batched throughput alone.

```text
patch_table.tsv
spatial_factors.npz
 temporal_factors.npz
singular_values.npz
reconstruction_residual.npy or per-patch residuals
metadata.json
```

The exact storage may differ, but the adapter must map it into a canonical dataclass and validate all shapes.

### 8.3 Import validation

The adapter must verify:

- patch coverage and bounds;
- factor dimensional compatibility;
- finite values;
- rank consistency;
- original-space reconstruction closure;
- frame and coordinate alignment;
- deterministic overlap-add reconstruction.

If closure cannot be verified, the imported result remains an external baseline and cannot initialize the semantic factorization.

### 8.4 No silent inference

Do not guess patch geometry, centering, rank, scale, or factor orientation from array shape alone. Return a structured validation failure.

## 13. Joint quiet-noise model

### 13.1 Construction

For each patch and quiet sample, collect aligned multiscale residual coordinates:

\[
q_i=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_i^{(5)}\\
q_i^{(7)}\\
q_i^{(15)}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Estimate:

- robust center;
- joint covariance;
- covariance eigenvalues and condition number;
- scale-pair correlations;
- temporal autocorrelation;
- spatial autocorrelation;
- intensity-conditioned variance;
- optional low-dimensional joint Parzen reference.

### 13.2 Mandatory models

1. `joint_covariance_robust` — mandatory reference.
2. `joint_covariance_plus_parzen` — mandatory ITL residual reference.
3. `independent_scale_noise` — ablation only.

### 13.3 Residual qualification

A residual may be renamed `qualified_measurement_noise` only when it passes all configured checks:

- joint held-out quiet CS divergence;
- covariance calibration;
- temporal ACF bounds;
- spatial ACF bounds;
- no material event locking on injected truth;
- no coherent motion-edge or annular neural structure;
- finite uncertainty and closure.

Otherwise retain `noise_candidate`.

## 14. Optimization procedure

### 14.1 Initialization

For every patch:

1. construct the three scale views;
2. obtain local PCA factors;
3. align factor signs and order across views by absolute correlation and spatial/temporal compatibility;
4. initialize a compact shared group from 5/7 agreement;
5. initialize a broad group from 15-pixel shared energy;
6. initialize artifact evidence from motion-edge and acquisition diagnostics;
7. leave unresolved dimensions in the private residual.

These initializations are label-free and are not final assignments.

### 14.2 Alternating reference

Implement a bounded alternating procedure:

1. update scale loadings by regularized least squares;
2. update shared latent groups by projected gradient;
3. update private residuals by reconstruction difference;
4. evaluate nuisance residualization;
5. evaluate group dependence and joint residual terms;
6. apply gradient clipping;
7. normalize scale/loadings to remove trivial scale ambiguity;
8. align components to the preceding valid iteration;
9. stop on objective and subspace-angle tolerance;
10. fall back when the fit becomes non-finite or semantically unresolved.

### 14.3 Numerical conditioning is not independence

Within-group orthogonalization may be used for numerical conditioning, but reports must not interpret orthogonal coordinates as independent physical sources.

### 14.4 Restarts and selection

Use the frozen seeds `[7, 13, 19]` for the first comparison.

Select the fit without labels using, in order:

1. valid closure;
2. finite and converged status;
3. held-out quiet residual score;
4. overlap consistency;
5. objective value;
6. deterministic seed as the final tie breaker.

### 14.5 Fallbacks

Supported statuses:

```text
resolved
ambiguous
fallback_orthogonal
fallback_independent_pca
failed
```

Do not force a neural/background assignment when the assignment margin is below threshold.

## 18. Scientific gates

The following are default v1 gates. The manifest may tighten them but may not silently weaken them after results are seen.

### C0 — implementation integrity

Pass when:

- focused unit and integration tests pass;
- all public interfaces serialize deterministically;
- preflight validates paths, shapes, checksums, frame indexing, coordinates, RAM, disk, and output collisions;
- label projection overlay is correct;
- no generated or completed output is overwritten.

### C1 — numerical reconstruction

Pass when:

- all required generated fixtures are finite;
- normalized p99 closure error is at most `1e-5`;
- normalized maximum closure error is at most `1e-4`;
- no patch has a zero overlap-add denominator;
- fallback behavior is deterministic.

### C2 — generated attribution

Pass when the full ITL model, relative to the orthogonal shared/private baseline:

- reduces total signal leakage into background, artifact, and noise candidate;
- does not increase background/artifact leakage into signal by more than the preregistered tolerance;
- improves diagonality of the B/S/A/N attribution matrix;
- passes on compact, broad-neural, correlated-neural, motion, and heteroscedastic cases rather than only one morphology.

The initial recommended improvement threshold is 5% relative on the primary leakage score. Report the raw values even if the threshold is not met.

### C3 — signal preservation

Default generated/semi-synthetic requirements:

- median peak-amplitude ratio in `[0.90, 1.10]`;
- median temporal-area ratio in `[0.85, 1.15]`;
- median peak-time error at most 1 frame;
- p95 peak-time error at most 3 frames;
- no morphology subgroup may lose more than the preregistered tolerance relative to the best safe baseline.

### C4 — residual qualification

Pass when:

- held-out quiet joint CS divergence improves over the independent-scale residual;
- covariance and ACF calibration pass;
- injected event energy remaining in the noise candidate is below the configured limit;
- no coherent neural morphology remains in the residual above the quiet null;
- no residual is called measurement noise before this gate passes.

### C5 — stability

Pass when:

- all declared seeds converge or use a valid fallback;
- matched shared-subspace correlation is at least 0.90 median;
- group assignment is stable across seeds and nearby bandwidths;
- no best-run-only claim is made when failure rate is material.

### C6 — real-data utility

A decomposition may advance as an auxiliary feature when:

- it improves held-out budget-20 or budget-40 recall in at least three of four bursts, or improves exhaustive-field precision at fixed recall;
- budget-58 recall is non-inferior within the preregistered tolerance;
- carrier timing and amplitude remain within C3 bounds;
- candidate burden is reported without calling it precision.

### C7 — scientific-carrier promotion

The reconstructed structured signal may replace the current scientific carrier only after C0--C6 pass and exhaustive-field review finds no material signal deletion or hallucination.

Until then:

```text
scientific trace = accepted carrier
new decomposition = candidate reconstruction and auxiliary evidence
```

### C8 — real-time candidacy

Pass only after a dedicated causal/frozen benchmark demonstrates:

- fixed memory;
- bounded fallback;
- p95 total software time below the 20 ms frame period;
- no use of future frames;
- separate calibration/adaptation timing.

Batched offline frames per second do not satisfy this gate.

## 25. Reference pseudocode

```python
def fit_dependent_multiscale_patch(
    patch_views: dict[str, np.ndarray],
    quiet_views: dict[str, np.ndarray],
    nuisance: np.ndarray | None,
    config: DependentMultiscaleConfig,
    seed: int,
) -> tuple[DependentMultiscaleFit, PatchDecomposition]:
    # 1. Local factorization in each aligned scale view.
    factors = {
        view_id: fit_local_factorization(values, config.factorization)
        for view_id, values in patch_views.items()
    }

    # 2. Joint quiet-noise model retains cross-scale dependence.
    quiet_model = fit_joint_quiet_noise_model(
        quiet_views,
        factors=factors,
        config=config.noise,
    )

    # 3. Label-free compact/broad/artifact initialization.
    state = initialize_semantic_groups(
        factors=factors,
        quiet_model=quiet_model,
        nuisance=nuisance,
        config=config.model,
        seed=seed,
    )

    # 4. Alternating bounded updates.
    for iteration in range(config.optimization.max_iterations):
        state = update_loadings(state, factors, config)
        state = update_latents(state, factors, nuisance, config)
        state = update_private_residuals(state, factors)
        state = normalize_ambiguities(state)
        state = align_to_previous(state)

        loss = reconstruction_loss(state, factors, quiet_model)
        loss += group_dependence_loss(state, nuisance, config.information)
        loss += joint_quiet_residual_loss(state, quiet_model, config.information)
        loss += structural_regularization(state, config.regularization)

        if not np.isfinite(loss):
            return fallback_patch(...)
        if converged(state, loss, config.optimization):
            break

    # 5. Map semantic groups back to observation coordinates.
    decomposition = reconstruct_original_patch(
        state=state,
        factors=factors,
        original_patch=patch_views[config.observation_view_id],
    )

    # 6. Verify closure and qualify the residual.
    validate_patch_closure(decomposition, config.evaluation)
    residual_status = qualify_noise_candidate(
        decomposition.noise_candidate,
        quiet_model,
        state,
        config.noise,
    )

    fit = finalize_fit(state, residual_status, seed, config)
    return fit, decomposition
```

The real implementation may differ internally, but it must preserve the visible stages and diagnostics.

## 26. Failure modes and required behavior

### Scale collapse

**Failure:** all signal is assigned to one view or group because of arbitrary factor scaling.  
**Required response:** normalize loadings/latents, report scale energy, and fall back when condition numbers exceed bounds.

### Broad-neural misclassification

**Failure:** any 15-pixel response is labeled background.  
**Required response:** include the broad legitimate neural fixture and allow neural scale signatures with strong broad support.

### Correlated-neuron suppression

**Failure:** coordinated neurons are rotated apart or attenuated to reduce dependence.  
**Required response:** groupwise penalties only; preserve neural within-group dependence; report correlated-neuron fixture performance.

### Residual whitening masquerading as denoising

**Failure:** residuals appear decorrelated but contain event-locked neural energy.  
**Required response:** residual event-locking and attribution gates; retain `noise_candidate` name.

### Patch seams

**Failure:** neighboring patches produce conflicting channels.  
**Required response:** overlap consistency loss, seam diagnostics, deterministic blending, and fallback.

### Motion leakage

**Failure:** motion edges enter structured signal.  
**Required response:** nuisance conditioning, artifact group, motion-edge diagnostics, and unresolved status.

### Provider-factor ambiguity

**Failure:** imported factors cannot reconstruct or lack metadata.  
**Required response:** treat the provider output as an external baseline only.

### Objective dominance

**Failure:** one information term overwhelms reconstruction or structural terms.  
**Required response:** log normalized term magnitudes, enforce bounded weights, and include zero-weight baselines.

### Best-seed reporting

**Failure:** one seed looks good while others fail.  
**Required response:** report failure rate, matched subspaces, and seed-stable selection.

### Proposal dilution

**Failure:** adding many scale/component proposals lowers top-k performance.  
**Required response:** separate native proposals, identical-union ranking, source quotas, and oracle ceiling.

## 27. Explicit non-goals

The v1 implementation must not claim to:

- identify one ICA component per neuron;
- prove uniqueness of the physical B/S/A/N decomposition;
- infer measurement noise solely because a residual is visually unstructured;
- make neurons independent;
- make deterministic scale transforms independent;
- replace motion correction;
- replace the accepted scientific carrier before promotion gates pass;
- infer precision from sparse-positive labels;
- prove real-time operation from batched throughput;
- recover information absent from the observation without a declared prior;
- generalize from calcium to voltage without changing the temporal and sign constraints.

## 31. Final implementation principle

The architectural invariant is:

```text
one measured movie
  -> several aligned scale views
  -> dependent semantic subspaces
  -> one reversible original-space B/S/A/N decomposition
  -> qualified residual and uncertainty
  -> auxiliary proposal/ranking evidence
  -> scientific carrier retained until promotion gates pass
```

The method is not “ICA at several scales.” It is a structured model in which:

- the same source is allowed to remain dependent across scales;
- coordinated neural sources are allowed to remain dependent;
- common causes are modeled or conditioned out;
- only inappropriate between-group dependence is penalized;
- the residual must match measured quiet acquisition statistics;
- every claimed separation is accountable in original observation coordinates.
