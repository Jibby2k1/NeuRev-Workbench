\begin{abstract}
Calcium-imaging pipelines are often evaluated against incomplete annotations,
so unmatched detections cannot safely be treated as false positives and local
recentering can cross neuronal identities. We developed an identifiability-first
evaluation framework and applied it to one zebrafish fluorescence recording
containing \VenueCohortBursts{} burst intervals,
\VenueCohortOccurrences{} confirmed neuron--burst occurrences, and
\VenueCohortSites{} immutable coordinate-defined sites. At the frozen per-lane
\(B=\VenuePerLaneBudget\) operating point, each of three candidate lists was
evaluated separately; their any-lane union recovered
\VenueStrictRecovered/\VenueCohortOccurrences{} occurrences. A separate
canonical-collapsed sensitivity analysis recovered
\VenueCanonicalRecovered/\VenueCanonicalOccurrences. Identity-aware analysis
of the \VenueStrictMissed{} all-lane misses found
\VenueIdentityCollision{} apparent recentering gains that collided with an
existing labeled identity and \VenueIdentityClear{} identity-clear hypotheses
absent from all audited proposal lists within eight pixels through per-lane
\(B=\VenueCounterfactualBudget\). In a distinct detector-selected batch of
\VenueReviewedCandidates{} previously unmatched sites, one blinded reviewer
called \VenueReviewDefinite{} definite and \VenueReviewProbable{} probable
neurons; this selected-batch yield is not detector precision. Raw fluorescence
led event-versus-guarded-quiet retrieval (site-weighted AUC \VenueRawAUC,
95\% interval \VenueRawAUCLow--\VenueRawAUCHigh). Exact-truth simulations
supported complementary amplitude and spatial-context roles while exposing
close-source identity and deployable-stopping limits. The framework separates
proposal, ranking, suppression, identity, and review failures without claiming
full-field precision or cross-recording generalization.
\end{abstract}
