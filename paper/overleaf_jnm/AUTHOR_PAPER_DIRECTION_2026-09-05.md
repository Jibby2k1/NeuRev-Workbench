## Recommended paper

### Working title

**Identity-safe evaluation of neuron detection in sparse-positive zebrafish
calcium imaging**

Alternative, if the candidate-review result remains central after independent
adjudication:

**Identity-safe evaluation reveals candidate neurons and distinct detection
failure modes in zebrafish calcium imaging**

### One-sentence contribution

This study shows how to evaluate and interpret neuron-detection pipelines when
annotations are incomplete: preserve spatial identity, treat unmatched
candidates as unknown, and separate proposal absence, ranking, non-maximum
suppression, and identity collision before claiming biological recovery.

### Draft abstract

Calcium-imaging pipelines are often evaluated against incomplete annotations,
so unmatched detections cannot safely be treated as false positives and local
recentering can cross neuronal identities. We developed an identifiability-first
evaluation framework and applied it to one zebrafish fluorescence recording
containing four burst intervals, 106 confirmed neuron--burst occurrences, and 50
immutable coordinate-defined sites. At the frozen per-lane B58 operating point,
each of three candidate lists was evaluated separately; their any-lane union
recovered 94/106 occurrences. A distinct canonical-collapsed sensitivity
analysis recovered 93/102. Identity-aware analysis of the 12 occurrences missed
by all evaluated lanes showed that eight apparent recentering gains collided
with an existing labeled identity, whereas four were identity-clear hypotheses
absent from all audited proposal lists within eight pixels through per-burst
B100. In a separate blinded single-reviewer assessment of 18 previously
unmatched detector sites,
9 were called definite neurons and 4 probable neurons. This 13/18 fraction
describes an enriched selected batch, not detector precision. Raw fluorescence
led the harder event-versus-quiet retrieval assay (site-weighted AUC 0.931,
95% interval 0.906--0.952). Registered exact-truth simulations supported
complementary amplitude and spatial-context roles while exposing close-source
identity and deployable-stopping limits. These results argue for identity-safe,
positive--unlabeled evaluation and competition-aware proposal resolution, while
leaving full-field precision and cross-recording generalization unresolved.

This draft intentionally says “candidate” or “hypothesis,” not “new validated
neuron,” until independent review and adjudication are complete.

## The central story

The strongest manuscript is an evaluation and measurement paper, not a claim
that a new detector has decisively won.

1. **The label problem precedes the model problem.** The known annotations are
   sparse positives. Unmatched candidates are unknown, and standard precision,
   specificity, and false-positive claims are unidentified without bounded
   exhaustive review.
2. **Measurement representation is not biological identity.** Raw, temporal
   ICA, local standardization, coherence, recurrence, and related maps expose
   different aspects of the signal. Two-frame ICA is largely derivative-like;
   stable reconstructed ICA output does not make individual filters named
   neuronal sources.
3. **Known-positive recovery is strong but incomplete.** At per-lane B58, the
   any-lane union recovers 94/106 occurrences. Raw leads frame-level retrieval,
   while spatial/contextual features contribute localized structure and
   candidate-ranking value.
4. **The unmatched-candidate review broadens the observable morphology.** A
   selected batch contained 9 definite and 4 probable neuron calls among 18
   previously unmatched sites, including small, low-SNR, discreet, crescent,
   and possibly multi-source appearances. This is discovery-generating evidence,
   not an estimate of precision.
5. **Most apparent localization repairs are identity errors.** Eight of 12
   local recentering gains crossed into already labeled identities. The four
   identity-clear cases are proposal-absence hypotheses, not ranking misses at
   the audited outputs.
6. **Exact truth clarifies feature roles without claiming biological transfer.**
   The combined stack led the 432-movie compact-budget benchmark, spatial context
   led all six held generator families, and close unequal-amplitude sources plus
   source-count-free stopping remained explicit failure boundaries.
7. **The methodological contribution is the disciplined decomposition.** The
   paper contributes an identity contract, positive--unlabeled evaluation,
   frozen operating budgets, matched controls, grouped uncertainty, review
   provenance, and explicit scientific-promotion gates.

## Claims to avoid

- “The detector has 72.2% precision.” The 13/18 result is from an enriched,
  selected candidate batch.
- “Thirteen new neurons were discovered.” These are provisional likely-neuron
  calls from one reviewer; one-to-one identity and independent adjudication are
  incomplete.
- “ICA recovered neuronal sources.” The evidence supports a stable measurement
  representation, not source identity.
- “The augmented atlas improves performance.” The point estimate improved, but
  the grouped interval crosses zero and scientific promotion is false.
- “The method generalizes.” Only one recording has been evaluated.
- “Displaced or quiet samples are negatives.” Their biological state is unknown.

## Recommended main-paper structure

### 1. Introduction

Frame the unmet need as evaluation under sparse-positive annotation and crowded
spatial identity. End with three questions: what is recoverable at frozen known
sites, what do unmatched candidates represent, and which failure stage limits
additional recovery?

### 2. Materials and methods

Lead with the observation/site/canonical-identity contract, annotation and frame
semantics, frozen candidate budgets, and positive--unlabeled estimands. Then
describe the representation panel, matched spatial and temporal controls,
blinded candidate review, identity-collision audit, and grouped uncertainty.
Keep operator derivations and the larger ICA/whitening program in the supplement.

### 3. Results

Use this order:

1. recording and immutable evaluation cohort;
2. shared event waveform and representation behavior;
3. known-positive retrieval and localized controls;
4. provisional unmatched-candidate findings;
5. identity-aware decomposition of the 12 all-lane misses;
6. exact-truth feature-role synthesis and unresolved failure boundaries.

Feature Atlas and other late engineering screens remain outside the main claim
path until they are registered, evidence-bound, and scientifically audited.

### 4. Discussion

Center the discussion on why incomplete labels and crowded identities can make
apparently favorable detector metrics scientifically misleading. Discuss the
new candidates as a broadened hypothesis space, not a neuron census. Position
competition-aware one-to-one resolution, independent review, bounded-field
truth, and independent-recording transfer as the next steps.

### 5. Conclusion

Conclude that reliable discovery requires preserving identity and uncertainty
through the evaluation pipeline. Avoid ending on ICA alone; the transferable
contribution is the evaluation framework.

## Main-figure plan

The present manuscript is analytically rich but too diffuse for a first-reading
story. A six-figure main paper would be clearer.

1. **Evaluation problem and identity contract:** recording, four burst windows,
   106 occurrences, 50 sites, sparse-positive labels, and error stages.
2. **Measurement representations:** stage-specific response-maximizing
   Raw/ICA/local-standardization images, pooled traces, and the exploratory
   38-site/12-site measurement-phenotype view.
3. **Frozen known-positive evaluation:** retrieval AUC, B58 recovery, spatial
   displacement controls, and claim boundaries.
4. **Previously unmatched candidates:** blinded-review design and representative
   definite, probable, uncertain, and artifact/neighbor-confounded cases.
5. **Identity-aware miss decomposition:** 8 collisions versus 4 identity-clear
   hypotheses, with proposal/ranking/NMS/identity flow.
6. **Exact-truth validation:** factorial and held-generator-family comparisons,
   morphology intervention, and explicit close-source/stopping limits.

The Feature Atlas visualization is retained only as a presubmission audit asset;
it is not included in either six-figure manuscript package.

Move trace taxonomies, ICA filter/reconstruction stability, denominator
reproduction, operator-frequency response, extended perturbations, and detailed
ablation tables to the supplement unless a target editor requests otherwise.

## Venue strategy

### 1. Journal of Neuroscience Methods -- recommended first submission

This is the best fit for the current evidence because the contribution is a
neuroscience measurement and evaluation method, and the existing package is
already structured for this journal. The paper can be valuable without claiming
population biology or a universally superior detector. Before submission, add
the independently adjudicated candidate review, or make the single-reviewer
status unmistakable in the title, abstract, figures, and limitations.

Official journal page:
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-neuroscience-methods>

### 2. Neuroinformatics -- strong alternative after a public software/data release

This venue is attractive if the manuscript foregrounds the reusable identity
schema, evidence registry, review tooling, artifact contracts, and software.
Its scope explicitly emphasizes data structures and software tools for analysis,
modeling, integration, and sharing in neuroscience, including independent tests
and evaluations. A public, documented release and portable example data would be
important for this version.

Official scope:
<https://link.springer.com/journal/12021/aims-and-scope>

### 3. Imaging Neuroscience -- plausible but needs a broader imaging-method case

The journal includes major advances in brain-imaging methods across humans and
other species and offers Research, Software Toolbox, and Technical Note formats.
It becomes compelling if the framework is demonstrated on an independent
recording or packaged as a broadly reusable optical-imaging toolbox. Its current
guide also expects a clear data/code availability statement; the listed APC is
US$1,600, subject to the journal's waiver policy.

Official scope and guide:
<https://direct.mit.edu/imag>
<https://direct.mit.edu/imag/pages/guide_for_authors>

### 4. Frontiers in Neuroinformatics -- practical open-access fallback

This is a reasonable route if the emphasis becomes the computational workflow,
review system, and reproducible analysis tooling. The stated scope includes
analytical tools for experimental neuroscience data and explicitly encourages
data and code sharing. It is less selective as a venue strategy but directly
aligned with a tool-centered paper.

Official scope:
<https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroinformatics/about>

### Stretch target: PLOS Computational Biology

Do not make this the first submission on the present evidence. Its Methods
articles require an outstanding method with broad adoption potential and strong
biological relevance. It becomes realistic after independent-recording transfer,
an adjudicated bounded truth set, a broadly usable software release, and a clear
advance over standard calcium-imaging evaluation practice.

Official scope:
<https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/s/journal-information>
